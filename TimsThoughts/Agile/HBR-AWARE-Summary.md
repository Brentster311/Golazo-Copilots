# HBR AWARE Framework — Article Summary

**Citation**: Hermann, E., Puntoni, S., & Morewedge, C. K. (2026, March–April). Why gen AI feels so threatening to workers. *Harvard Business Review*. https://hbr.org/2026/03/why-gen-ai-feels-so-threatening-to-workers

**Accessed**: 2026-04-07

---

## Core Argument

Gen AI threatens three fundamental psychological needs that workers rely on for motivation and well-being: **competence** (feeling effective), **autonomy** (feeling in control), and **relatedness** (feeling meaningfully connected). When these needs are satisfied, workers embrace AI as a tool. When frustrated, they resist, disengage, or actively sabotage adoption.

Key data points:
- 45% of CEOs report most employees are resistant or hostile to gen AI (Kyndryl, 2025)
- 85% of leaders / 78% of managers use gen AI regularly; only 51% of workers do (BCG, 2025)
- 31% of knowledge workers admit to actively working against their company's AI initiatives (Writer, 2025)
- 54% would use AI tools without formal approval; 32% already keep AI use hidden from employer

---

## The Three Psychological Needs

### Competence
- AI boosts capability (89% of senior leaders say it enhances employee skills)
- But also threatens: redefines required capabilities, may atrophy entry-level skill development paths
- Risk: if AI automates the entry-level work through which people build expertise, career ladders break

### Autonomy
- AI can free workers from repetitive tasks → more focus on meaningful work
- But mandating AI use (as Microsoft, Shopify) or holding workers accountable for AI-generated output creates "algorithmic cages" — standardized procedures that strip task ownership
- Workers can feel cast in a supporting role to the technology

### Relatedness
- AI can foster connection when it enables more time for human interaction
- But automating collaborative tasks can create loneliness, reduce team cohesion, and amplify generational gaps
- Workers also show bias: their own AI use feels legitimate; others' does not

## The AWARE Framework (Hermann, Puntoni & Morewedge)

> **A**cknowledge · **W**atch · **A**lign · **R**edesign · **E**mpower

### A — Acknowledge
Proactively create space for open dialogue about how gen AI affects tasks, roles, and self-worth.
Surface concerns; demonstrate that workers' interests are valued. Builds psychological safety and reduces quiet resistance.

### W — Watch
Identify whether workers are using **adaptive** coping (skill enhancement, workflow adjustment, peer collaboration) or **maladaptive** coping (task avoidance, withdrawal, shadow AI use, active sabotage).
Monitor adoption metrics; intervene with empathy before motivation or performance declines.

### A — Align
Design training, mentoring, job design, and feedback to meet workers' psychological needs — not one-size-fits-all.
Examples: PwC My AI initiative, Citigroup AI Champions, personalized learning pathways.
Only 36% of employees feel properly trained; 52% of IT decision-makers don't know how to train employees on gen AI.

### R — Redesign
End-to-end workflow redesign (not plug-and-play tool deployment) produces higher effectiveness.
Division of labor principle: give gen AI repetitive/data-heavy tasks; give humans empathy, creativity, critical thinking, and ethical judgment.
Redesign is ongoing — as AI capabilities evolve, so must workflows.

### E — Empower
Transparency about what AI will do, how decisions are made, and how roles may evolve.
Involve workers in implementation — they know where AI value is highest within their business units.
Build inclusive access across all roles, departments, and seniority levels (BNY example: 60% of workforce onboarded, 5,000 built their own agents).

## Important Note: Two Different AWARE Frameworks

Tim Mallalieu's document (SRC-007) attributes its framework to HBR and also calls it AWARE, but uses a completely different expansion:

| Source | AWARE Expansion | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Hermann et al. (2026) — this article | Acknowledge · Watch · Align · Redesign · Empower | Psychological change management for workers adapting to gen AI |
| Tim Mallalieu SRC-007 | Align · Workforce · Architecture · Responsibility · Execution | Workflow/delivery transformation for embedding AI in mission teams |

These are not the same framework. Tim references HBR's general AI transformation body of work but applies his own expansion of the acronym — likely adapted deliberately to fit a technical mission team context rather than a people-management context.

**Implication for response to Tim**: The HBR article (this source) offers a complementary lens. Where Tim's AWARE is operationally focused (what to build and how), the HBR AWARE is psychologically focused (how workers respond to the change). Both are needed for a real transformation to succeed.
