## My View: Golazo, Delivery, and What Matters

Author: Brent Jensen
Date: 2026-03-14

### Core Position

My view is that software delivery performance is primarily a learning-speed problem, not just a coordination problem.

The companies that understand customer problems and solve them the fastest will win.

This has always been true.

What has changed is the required speed. With AI tools, that speed bar has increased by at least an order of magnitude, similar to the shift from packaged products to online services.

I believe teams win when they:
- Understand customer problems deeply.
- Reduce cycle time from idea to validated solution.
- Work in small slices with fast feedback.

### Where I Differ From a Delivery-Only Model

I agree that execution discipline matters. I do not agree that accountability structures alone solve delivery failure.

If we optimize mostly for initiative coordination, we can accidentally create:
- More queue time.
- More reporting overhead.
- Slower learning loops.

In complex product systems, this leads to motion without enough customer value.

We need to stop building things in isolation. We need to build things that build things, and build them well.

### My Diagnostic Lens

When delivery slows down, I assume first that the system has:
- Large batches.
- Hidden dependencies.
- Excess WIP.
- Long wait states between steps.

I do not start with blame. I start with flow.

### What Golazo Optimizes

Golazo is designed to optimize:
- Value discovery speed.
- Learning flow.
- Cycle time to useful outcomes.

Practically, that means:
- Small work increments.
- Shared understanding before heavy build.
- Tight feedback loops.
- Continuous reduction of rework and queue time.

### How I Think About Accountability

I support outcome accountability.

That means:
- Clear responsibility for customer and platform outcomes.
- Fast escalation when blocked.
- No ambiguity about who drives decisions.

But accountability should increase learning and delivery speed, not become a proxy for control theater.

You get more of what you incentivize. If you want team players, you need to incentivize teams.

### Decision Rule I Prefer

For any process, role, or governance step, I ask:

1. Does this reduce time to validated customer value?
2. Does this reduce queue time or just document it?
3. Does this improve learning rate in the system?
4. Does this help teams ship safely with less friction?

If the answer is no, we should simplify or remove it.

All outcomes should be measured from the customer perspective and reflect a real customer problem addressed.

No vanity KPIs. No watermelon metrics.

### What Good Looks Like

A high-performing engineering organization should show:
- Faster cycle time.
- Better customer outcomes.
- Lower avoidable toil.
- Smaller and safer releases.
- Better reliability without slower flow.

Quality is defined by customers, not by us. It is defined by the degree to which we solve their problems.

To solve customer problems, we must understand those problems in near real time.

The transition we learned during Product-to-Services still applies: testing matters, but testers as a separate handoff function do not. High-performing systems integrate quality into the flow.

What we are learning now is similar: coding matters, but coders as a silo do not. System capability, learning speed, and end-to-end outcomes matter more than role boundaries.

### Bottom Line

Delivery is important, but delivery without rapid learning can optimize the wrong thing.

My stance is simple:
Build an adaptive system that learns fast, ships in small increments, and measures success by customer-impacting outcomes over time.

Golazo and Kanban are strong at team-based dynamics. One of their weaknesses is that they do not naturally optimize for individual goals outside of the backlog.
