When I said Golazo, SAFe, and the manifesto occupy different parts of the delivery problem space, I meant something quite specific.

They are solving three different optimization problems, even though they all talk about “delivery”.

If you put them on the same conceptual map, it becomes clearer why the philosophies feel so different.

The Three Axes of Software Delivery Systems

Most delivery systems optimize one of three dominant constraints:

Flow efficiency (how quickly work moves through the system)

Coordination reliability (how well many teams align to deliver large outcomes)

Engineering adaptability (how easily the system can evolve technically)

Different frameworks emphasize different axes.

1. Golazo — Flow Optimization

Golazo is fundamentally a flow optimization system.

Its mechanisms aim to reduce delays in the work stream:

small proposals

shared understanding

fewer review surprises

smaller batches

faster merges

The system objective looks like this:

maximize value delivered / calendar time


Or equivalently:

minimize cycle time


This aligns with ideas from Donald Reinertsen and Lean product development.

So Golazo sits here:

FLOW OPTIMIZATION

2. SAFe — Coordination Optimization

SAFe exists primarily to solve a different problem:

large organization
+
many teams
+
shared platform
=
coordination chaos


SAFe introduces structures to synchronize teams:

Agile Release Trains

Program Increment planning

architectural roles

portfolio alignment

The objective becomes:

maximize reliability of large initiative delivery


Not necessarily minimizing cycle time for small changes.

So SAFe sits here:

COORDINATION OPTIMIZATION

3. The Manifesto — Governance Optimization

The manifesto appears to target a slightly different constraint again.

It tries to fix perceived organizational problems like:

unclear responsibility

stalled initiatives

hidden blockers

lack of execution discipline

Its mechanisms are mostly governance tools:

accountable leads

single plan of record

escalation discipline

weekly proof of progress

The objective becomes:

maximize execution accountability


This is not exactly flow or coordination — it is organizational governance.

So the manifesto sits here:

GOVERNANCE OPTIMIZATION

Each approach pulls the system toward a different equilibrium.

Why the Tension Feels So Strong

Because optimizing one axis often degrades another.

Examples:

More governance

→ more coordination overhead
→ slower flow

More autonomy and flow

→ less centralized coordination

More scaling structure

→ less adaptability

So when you read the manifesto through a flow optimization lens, it feels like the system is adding friction.

But from a leadership perspective focused on initiative reliability, those same structures look helpful.

The Interesting Part

High-performing organizations actually balance all three forces.

For example:

Layer	Focus
Engineering practices	adaptability
Team work flow	cycle time
Organizational structure	coordination

Problems arise when one layer tries to compensate for failures in another.

For instance:

governance mechanisms compensating for poor flow

coordination structures compensating for unclear architecture

The Key Insight

Golazo, SAFe, and the manifesto are not directly competing frameworks.

They address different constraints in the same system.

The real question is:

Which constraint is actually limiting delivery in your organization?


That determines which approach produces the largest improvement.

If you'd like, there’s actually one very surprising observation about Golazo once you place it on this map:

It quietly attacks two of the three constraints simultaneously, which is unusual for delivery frameworks.
