## SRC-010
- Source type: journal article (magazine)
- Author(s): Erik Hermann; Stefano Puntoni; Carey K. Morewedge
- Year / date: 2026, March–April
- Title: Why Gen AI Feels So Threatening to Workers
- Container / publisher: Harvard Business Review
- DOI: none listed
- URL: https://hbr.org/2026/03/why-gen-ai-feels-so-threatening-to-workers
- Access date (if needed): 2026-04-07
- Notes / relevant quotes:
	- Introduces the HBR AWARE framework: Acknowledge · Watch · Align · Redesign · Empower — a change-management model for helping workers psychologically adapt to gen AI.
	- Core thesis: Gen AI threatens three psychological needs — competence, autonomy, and relatedness. When satisfied, workers embrace AI; when frustrated, they resist, disengage, or sabotage adoption.
	- Key data: 45% of CEOs report most employees resistant/hostile to gen AI; 31% of workers admit actively working against AI initiatives; 54% would use AI without formal approval.
	- Adaptive vs. maladaptive coping: maladaptive behaviors include task avoidance, withdrawal, shadow AI use, and outright sabotage.
	- End-to-end workflow redesign outperforms plug-and-play tool deployment on all measured outcomes.
	- "Algorithmic cage" concept: mandating AI use with standardized procedures strips workers of autonomy and identity.
	- NOTE: Tim Mallalieu (SRC-007) also calls his framework AWARE and attributes it to HBR, but uses a different expansion (Align · Workforce · Architecture · Responsibility · Execution). These are two distinct frameworks sharing an acronym. Tim's is operationally focused; this article's is psychologically focused.
